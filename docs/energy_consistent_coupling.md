# Energy-consistent coupling of Hopfield memory and predictive-coding inference

## Summary

Treat the Hopfield memory as one more term of the energy that PC inference already
descends, instead of as a module that overwrites the hidden state:

```
E_total(z) = E_PC(z) + lam * E_H(z; M, beta)
z <- z - eta * ( dE_PC/dz + lam * (z - retrieved(z)) )          # joint inference step
```

* `dE_H/dz = z - softmax(beta z M^T) M = z - retrieved(z)` (analytic; `hopfield_energy_grad`).
* `lam = 0` is plain PC (the baseline). With no PC term and `eta * lam = 1`, one step is exactly
  one-step Hopfield retrieval.
* Proposal Method A (`h_new = PC_update + lam * Retrieval(h)`) adds the retrieved vector; the
  energy-consistent form pulls toward it (`lam * (retrieved - h)`). Written that way, Methods A and B
  are the same design: A is the gradient step of B.

## Stability rule

The Hessian of `E_H` is `I - beta * Cov_p(m)` (`Cov_p` = covariance of the memory vectors under the
softmax weights). Its eigenvalues lie in `[1 - beta * M_max^2, 1]`, so the **upper curvature of the
Hopfield term is exactly 1 for every beta and every memory**. Monotone descent only needs the upper
curvature, hence for gradient-descent inference with fixed memory:

```
eta < 2 / (L_PC + lam)          (eta <= 1 / (L_PC + lam) is the conservative choice)
```

where `L_PC` is the largest Hessian eigenvalue of `E_PC`. `beta` and the memory norm do not enter the
step-size limit; they set the negative curvature, i.e. how deep and separated the valleys are
(research question 6 of the proposal). `estimate_top_curvature` estimates `L_PC` for any
differentiable energy with Hessian-vector products (local estimate; re-estimate if `z` moves a lot).

Assumptions: fixed memory, `normalize=False` (retrieval and energy only match without score
normalization), full-batch gradient steps on `z`.

## Results (toy study, `benchmarks/pc_hopfield_toy.py` (run: `python -m benchmarks.pc_hopfield_toy`))

Linear chain, `d_in=32, d_h=16, d_o=4`, 6 prototypes, 200 noisy samples (`noise=0.5`), `beta=4`.
Output is smaller than the hidden layer, so the clamped output cannot pin down every hidden
coordinate. `L_PC = 2.433`; Hopfield curvature range `[-8, 1]`. This is a mechanism check, not
evidence about MNIST or FabricPC. The table was generated by a numpy port with identical data draws.

| lam | final E_PC | final E_total | mean distance to clean prototype | nearest-memory acc |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| start (forward pass) | 0.9533 | - | 3.0373 | 1.000 |
| 0 | 0.4630 | 0.4630 | 2.8119 | 1.000 |
| 0.05 | 0.4724 | 0.6873 | 2.6820 | 1.000 |
| 0.1 | 0.4971 | 0.8938 | 2.5638 | 1.000 |
| 0.3 | 0.6839 | 1.5818 | 2.1808 | 1.000 |
| 1 | 1.5155 | 3.0678 | 1.4373 | 1.000 |
| 3 | 2.8833 | 5.0881 | 0.7331 | 1.000 |

Reading: the pull moves the hidden state toward the stored prototype as designed, and it costs PC
energy (the network explains its input less well). Nearest-memory accuracy is already 1.0 without the
pull, so this toy shows **no downstream benefit**. Whether the trade-off pays off on a real task is the
open question for FabricPC experiments.

Step-size rule (300 inference steps): for `lam in {0.3, 1, 3}` the total energy decreases monotonically
at `0.5x` and `0.95x` of `2/(L_PC + lam)` and diverges at `1.05x` and `1.5x`. The measured top
curvature at the settled state equals `L_PC + lam`, so the limit is sharp in this setting.

## Limitations / open questions

* Quadratic `E_PC` only in the toy; nonlinear PC needs local curvature estimates.
* Fixed memory. Learnable or refreshed memory changes the energy during training.
* Uses full-batch inference; per-layer memory banks and the joint energy over several layers are the
  next step (sum of per-layer terms, one `lam_l` each).
* Related work: PC networks as associative memories (Salvatori et al., NeurIPS 2021), and work
  connecting PC to modern continuous Hopfield networks (NeurIPS 2022). Check novelty against these
  before claiming anything beyond the engineering of the coupling and the step-size rule.
